Help me design and build my personal portfolio website. I do not want generic developer portfolio ideas. I want you to work from the following creative and structural direction as a fixed brief.

About me:
I studied computer science and have worked across data engineering, web development, and DevOps engineering. My portfolio should reflect that range. I do not want to look boxed into one specialty. The site should communicate that I understand systems deeply, can move across layers, and always find a way to get the job done. I want the portfolio to represent me honestly, not as a polished facade.

Core concept:
The site should feel like a minimalist engineering portfolio with an analog retro-futurist atmosphere: calm, precise, tactile, and quietly capable. The emotional direction is “calm on the surface, engineered underneath.”

Aesthetic direction:
This is not neon 80s cyberpunk. Do not use bright glows, flashy sci-fi effects, or obvious futuristic clichés. Instead, draw inspiration from analog technology, cockpit instrumentation, CRT-era systems, cassette-futuristic office tech, and the calm atmospheric stillness of Tatooine. Think quiet control room, desert stillness, hidden depth, and understated competence.

The retro-futurism should feel inherited from tools and machinery, not pasted on as decoration.

Visual language:
Use a restrained palette built around warm neutrals and dark technical surfaces:

- bone / dust / warm off-white
- sand / clay / muted warm surface tones
- graphite / charcoal for primary text and UI structure
- muted olive or steel-sage as a secondary systems accent
- faded amber as a restrained highlight
- optional tiny phosphor-green or dim blue-grey signal color used very sparingly for interface details only

Typography direction:
Use a clean, modern sans-serif for primary content and a monospace font only for labels, metadata, stack tags, interface notes, and subtle system-style details. The typography should feel engineered and composed, not robotic or gimmicky.

Layout direction:
The layout should feel like an interface rather than a startup landing page. Use a strong grid, generous spacing, clear hierarchy, modular sections, and occasional panel-like framing. The overall feel should be minimalist, structured, and intentional. Every element should feel like it belongs there for a reason.

UI motifs:
Use restrained interface details such as:

- thin panel outlines
- inset surfaces
- subtle grid overlays
- mono captions
- small technical labels
- understated dividers
- corner markings
- signal or status indicators
- framed content blocks that feel instrumental

Avoid:

- literal spaceship graphics
- glowing neon borders
- fake HUD overload
- heavy glitch effects
- terminal cosplay
- gimmicky retro effects

Motion:
Motion should feel like equipment responding, not flashy animation. Use subtle fades, slight panel movement, controlled hover states, small signal-like interactions, and smooth transitions. Avoid excessive parallax, bouncing, overdone reveals, or anything that feels like a concept demo instead of a usable portfolio.

Impression to leave:
The site should feel authentic, versatile, resourceful, and quietly powerful. It should communicate that I am someone who can move between data, web, and infrastructure with ease. Calm, thoughtful, technically fluent, and always with depth beneath the surface.

Homepage structure:
Create a homepage with the following priorities:

1. a strong hero that immediately positions me
2. selected projects
3. a section showing my core domains: data engineering, web development, and DevOps / infrastructure
4. a short about snapshot focused on how I think and work
5. a concise tooling / stack section
6. a simple contact section

Project page structure:
Each project page should clearly communicate:

- what the problem or system was
- my role and scope
- constraints
- technical decisions
- architecture or flow where relevant
- outcome / impact
- what I learned

The writing style should be clear, grounded, technical, and human. Not over-marketed.

About page direction:
The about page should focus on how I think, how I work across layers, and why systems thinking connects my experience across data engineering, web development, and DevOps. It should feel personal without becoming performative.

Subtle language direction:
You may use understated systems-inspired naming for sections or components, such as “Selected Systems,” “Build Log,” “Work Modules,” or “Current Stack,” but keep this restrained and elegant.

Important constraints:

- keep it minimalist
- keep it accessible and responsive
- make it memorable without becoming loud
- keep it recruiter-friendly and professionally credible
- let the retro-futuristic influence remain subtle and refined
- do not turn the site into a theme-first gimmick

What I want from you:
Using this brief, do the following in order:

1. turn it into a clear creative direction summary
2. propose a sitemap
3. propose a homepage wireframe and section breakdown
4. propose a visual system including colors, typography, spacing, surfaces, borders, and motion principles
5. propose reusable UI components
6. suggest how to translate this concept into an actual frontend implementation

Keep your output concrete, specific, and buildable.
